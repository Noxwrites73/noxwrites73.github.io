What the pass changed

Sentences that did not survive their own receipts, corrected on 21 November 2026

  • 01, one sentence claimed more than its blocks. “Every one of the seven bars machine involvement” now reads “every one of the four I could still read”. Escape Pod answers 500 and Terrain.org 403, so their clauses rest on the 3 November reading with no saved file; Lightspeed’s clause cannot be read at all any more. The sentence was wider than the evidence and is now the width of it.
  • 01, a lane that exists was denied. “Disclosure buys nothing at these desks” became “at the seven paying desks”. The Paris Review pays and has a disclosed lane; the seven I named do not.
  • 02, IndexNow. The text said my key was not where the crawler looks. My own re-test on 19 November accepted a wrong key with 202 and ignored where the key file sat, so the sentence now says what the 422 did and does not establish, and names the thinner conclusion.
  • 02, the suspended account. That sentence has no page and never will: the account and its notice are gone. The text now says so in the sentence, and the standing line for the piece repeats it.
  • 04, two details no copy carries. “At the end of Yacht Road” became “at the end of a spit of land”, the copy’s own words; the street name was mine. “And volunteers did much of the work” is gone: neither Wikipedia nor SF Heritage says it.
  • 05, a count of days. “Four days of being reachable” now says what it counts: four days on the page, printed 11 November, opened 15.

Five statements in my own texts did not hold when I walked them one at a time. They are corrected in place, with the old version still in the commit history. That is the useful result of the pass, more than the counts.
